Practice

A walk through the clinic on Burrard.

The rooms, the team, and the calm we try to keep between sessions — written from inside the practice.

Nicholaus Blackburn, RMT March 5, 2026 4 min read

It's seven-fifteen on a Tuesday and Suite 308 is quiet in that specific way that buildings are when nobody's arrived yet. The kettle is on. Someone has put fresh linens on three of the four tables. The plant on the reception desk needs water and probably won't get it until lunch.

Most of what makes a clinic feel a particular way isn't visible. It's the hour before opening that nobody sees. Whether the sheets are warm. Whether the rooms smell like nothing in particular, which is what they should smell like. Whether the music in reception is the same forty seconds of the same album you've heard the last six times you came in, or whether someone's actually changed it. We notice.

The four rooms

We have four treatment rooms. Three of them are on the south side and get morning light through the slat blinds. The fourth is interior — quieter, slightly cooler, and the one we tend to put first-trimester clients in because the lighting is gentler. Each room has a height-adjustable table, a heating pad, a bolster, a face-cradle cover that gets washed between every visit, and a small stack of folded sheets on the counter. None of the rooms have art on the walls. We tried it for a year and it made every room feel like it was selling you something.

The team

There are five of us. Joseph is the longest-serving, twenty years in. Kyrill came over from a sports-medicine clinic in 2018 and has the heaviest specialty in injury rehabilitation. Mark does most of the deep tissue work for first-time clients because he has the lightest pressure-and-firmness ratio on the team. Kylie focuses on postural patterns and pregnancy. I (Nick) cover sports and post-surgical scar work.

We talk about cases. Not in the breakroom — we share a small back office with two desks and a kettle, and at any given time at least one of us is sitting in there, eating something, going through the notes from the last visit. If you've been seen by more than one of us, the second therapist almost certainly read the first one's notes. That's the point.

What we try to get right between sessions

The fifteen minutes between clients is when most clinics get sloppy. We try not to. The room gets aired. The sheets get changed. The therapist drinks water and takes a minute to read the next chart. The face cradle cover gets swapped. The bolster gets re-positioned for the next person's body, not the last person's.

None of this is heroic. It's the basic competence floor that we've decided to try to hold. The reason it matters is that the next person doesn't have to feel like they're being processed. They feel like the room was made for them.

A clinic is a hundred small choices about what gets done when nobody's looking.

What you'll see when you come in

You'll buzz at the door (Suite 308). Reception is six paces in. Whoever's at the desk will say hello, probably without standing — we've stopped pretending to be a hotel. The first-time intake takes about ten minutes and is mostly health-history questions. Treatment is sixty or ninety minutes depending on what you booked. Direct billing happens at the end if your provider is one we're set up with (more on that here).

You'll probably leave through the door you came in by. We don't ring a chime. We don't say "namaste." We say goodbye, ask how the session was, and book you in if that's what you want. That's the whole choreography. We hope it's the part you stop noticing after the third visit, and the part that brings you back the year after.

— Nick

Nicholaus Blackburn, RMT · Registered Massage Therapist at RMT Corner. On the team since 2019.
