Direct billing — what we can and can't do.

A primer on extended health coverage in BC and how the direct billing process actually works.

Joseph Lloyd Tweet, RMT February 20, 2026 5 min read

Direct billing is one of those things that's more tedious to explain than to use. The short version: most of our clients walk out without paying anything out of pocket, because their insurance covers the visit and we bill the insurer directly. The longer version is the one that matters when something doesn't go to plan — so here it is.

The insurers we're set up with

We currently direct-bill the following providers in British Columbia:

  • Pacific Blue Cross
  • Manulife (Cowan)
  • Sun Life
  • Canada Life (formerly Great-West Life)
  • Green Shield Canada
  • iA Financial Group
  • Desjardins Insurance
  • Equitable Life
  • ClaimSecure
  • ChamberPlan

If your insurer isn't on this list, we'll happily issue you a receipt with the regulatory codes (registration number, date, total) and you can submit it yourself. Most plans reimburse within five to ten business days that way.

What direct billing actually does

At the end of your session, we run the claim through your insurer in real time. The insurer responds with how much of the visit they'll cover, and we charge you only the remainder. No reimbursement waiting period; the math is settled before you leave reception.

It works almost every time. The exceptions are real but predictable.

When direct billing won't work

  • Your plan doesn't allow it. Some employer plans are configured for reimbursement only, even if your insurer otherwise supports direct billing. We can't override this.
  • You haven't been added to the plan yet. Common in the first month of a new job. The insurer's system doesn't know you exist.
  • You're at the annual cap. Most extended-health plans cap massage at $300–$1,500/year. Once you've hit it, the rest comes out of pocket until the plan year resets.
  • The session is over the per-visit limit. Some plans cover a fixed dollar amount per session (e.g. $80) regardless of treatment length. The rest is yours.
  • You don't have a doctor's note that the plan requires. This is rare but a few plans (mostly older Sun Life and ICBC plans) need a referral on file.

The plan is yours, not ours. We can read it; we can't change it.

What to bring

For your first visit, bring (or have ready on your phone):

  • The name of your insurer.
  • Your policy number (sometimes called plan number or group number).
  • Your certificate number (sometimes called member ID). This is the one that identifies you, not the plan.
  • A backup payment method, in case the claim doesn't go through.

If your spouse's plan also covers you, bring both. We can coordinate benefits so that one plan picks up what the other doesn't, in the right order. Most clients leave money on the table by not knowing this is possible.

ICBC and WorkSafeBC

Both are different from extended health and worth a separate note.

ICBC covers up to twenty-five massage therapy visits per claim, within twelve weeks of the date of your accident, with no doctor's referral required for the first set. We bill ICBC directly with your claim number. If you've been in a collision, get treatment started early — the coverage gets harder to access the longer you wait.

WorkSafeBC requires pre-approval and a referral from your doctor. We can't direct-bill until that's in place. If you've been injured at work, see your GP first.

The honest part

Insurance changes. Caps change. Plans get re-tendered. The list of providers we direct-bill grows; occasionally one drops off. If anything in this piece looks out of date, the front desk will know — and a quick call before you book will save you a billing surprise. Phone is faster than email for this.
