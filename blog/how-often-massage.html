How often should you book a massage?

The honest answer is it depends on what you're treating. Here's how we think about cadence.

Joseph Lloyd Tweet, RMT March 12, 2026 6 min read

This is the question we get more than any other, and the honest answer is that anybody who gives you a single number is selling something. Cadence depends on what you're treating. There is no "every two weeks" gospel. There is what your tissue needs, and there is the budget you can sustain, and the right answer sits in the overlap.

Here is how we actually think about it in clinic.

If you're treating something specific

Acute issues — a strained low back, a frozen shoulder mid-flare, a recent whiplash — usually want closer-spaced sessions for the first stretch. Two to three weekly visits over the first three weeks, then tapering as the tissue calms. The aim is to get on top of it before compensation patterns lock in.

Chronic issues — a long-running neck, recurring sciatica, an old surgical scar that has changed how you move — usually want fortnightly visits for the first six to eight weeks, then a monthly maintenance schedule once the system is calmer. Chronic tissue holds memory; the gap between sessions is where the pattern tries to come back.

If you're treating "I sit at a desk"

This is the most common reason people come in, and the most common one to over-prescribe for. For an otherwise healthy desk worker with no acute pain, every four to six weeks is plenty. More than that and you're paying for a result that you'd get anyway. Less than that — every eight to twelve weeks — works fine if you also have a movement habit (walking, lifting, swimming, cycling) that you actually do.

If you sit for ten hours a day and don't move outside it, monthly is probably the floor. Tissue that doesn't get loaded in any other way needs the input.

If you're an athlete

Endurance and combat athletes do best with weekly or fortnightly maintenance through hard training blocks, dropping to monthly in off-season. Strength athletes can usually go fortnightly through peak phases and monthly otherwise. Across the board: the session before a competition is rarely the right time to introduce new work. We keep race-week treatments light.

If you're pregnant

Pregnancy massage is a different animal. Most of our pregnant clients come monthly through the first trimester, fortnightly through the second, and weekly through the last six weeks. We adjust positioning and depth throughout. We do not work pregnant clients without confirmation that pregnancy is uncomplicated — please bring that conversation to us early.

If you're recovering from surgery

Surgical scar work usually starts six to eight weeks post-op (sometimes earlier, depending on the procedure and your surgeon's clearance). Weekly for the first month of treatment, then fortnightly, then monthly until the scar is mature. Total span is usually three to six months. The earlier we work the scar within safe limits, the better the long-term mobility.

Cadence isn't sacred. It's a hypothesis you adjust as the tissue tells you what it wants.

The cadence isn't the goal

What we're actually aiming for is the gap between sessions getting longer over time. If you started every two weeks and you're now comfortable at every six, that's a good outcome. If you've been on the same monthly cadence for three years and you'd be miserable without it, that's also fine — but it's worth asking, with your therapist, whether the work you're doing in between (or not doing) is part of the picture.

Massage isn't a substitute for the things massage doesn't replace: sleep, movement, water, food, time off your phone. It's an input. A good one, a useful one, but one input among several.

How we'll handle this with you

At your first visit we'll ask what you're treating, look at your tissue, and suggest a cadence that fits. Treat the suggestion as a hypothesis, not a prescription. We'll re-evaluate at every visit and shift the gap accordingly. If you want to come less often, we'll happily plan toward that. We'd rather have you for ten years than ten weeks.

— Joseph

Joseph Lloyd Tweet, RMT · Registered Massage Therapist and co-founder of RMT Corner. Twenty years in clinical practice in Vancouver.
